Fiji Cabinet Approves Six-Month Partnership With New Zealand Security Intelligence Service to Review Intelligence Structure

  • Fiji’s cabinet approved a six-month partnership with the New Zealand Security Intelligence Service (NZSIS), funded by Wellington, to assess Fiji’s intelligence structure and recommend reforms, Fiji’s government said in a statement on Wednesday.
  • Fiji did not identify a specific threat behind the review, according to the report. The NZSIS did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
  • The U.S. Embassy in Suva said earlier this week it had alerted Fiji and a regional organisation based in Suva to a suspected network compromise between July and August 2026, and said hackers linked to China maintained access to the organisation’s network for several days, according to the report.
  • China’s embassy in Suva rejected the allegations in a statement on Tuesday, saying Beijing opposed hacking and the politicisation of cybersecurity, according to the report.

Partnership, embassy alert and Chinese embassy response via the Fiji government, the U.S. Embassy in Suva and China’s embassy in Suva, cited in the original report. This card is based on the accessible portion only. Original headline: "New Zealand to help Fiji overhaul intelligence agencies".
