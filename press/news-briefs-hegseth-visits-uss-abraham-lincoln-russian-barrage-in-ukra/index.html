News briefs: Hegseth Visits USS Abraham Lincoln, Russian Barrage in Ukraine

  • Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth visited the USS Abraham Lincoln ahead of its return to San Diego today, taking responsibility for twice extending the crew’s record deployment, saying “I plead guilty” (The Guardian).
  • The carrier spent more than 260 straight days at sea without a port call, a stretch marked by sagging morale and eight suicide attempts across its strike group (AP).
  • A Russian barrage of roughly 70 missiles and dozens of drones killed at least 28 people across Ukraine on Wednesday, with 20 of the deaths in a single apartment building in the northern town of Pryluky; Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky noted the attack fell on Vladimir Putin’s birthday (Reuters).
  • The FBI said it foiled a planned mass shooting at the Mall of America, arresting an 18-year-old after he paid an undercover agent posing as an ISIS member for an assault rifle; the complaint names Sheikhdoon Abdullahi Mohamud, who told the informant he wanted to kill non-Muslims at the mall (AP).
  • Four astronauts who spent nearly eight months aboard the International Space Station are set to splash down off the California coast today; the mission lasted 237 days and circled Earth nearly 3,800 times (CBS News).
  • Christa Pike, believed to be the first person in the U.S. to survive after receiving lethal-injection drugs, is awake and talking after Tennessee’s failed execution attempt last week; she is battling pneumonia and cannot move her arms or swallow, her lawyers said Wednesday (CBS News).
  • France’s Henri Kagan, 95, and Japan’s Kenso Soai, 76, won the Nobel Prize in chemistry Wednesday for discovering how to make molecules come out in just one of their two mirror-image forms, work key to making safe medicines (Reuters).
  • Russia said it is willing to set up a Trump–Putin call about the death of the Siberian plague-lab worker, as the U.S. and WHO press Moscow for answers; Russian health officials blame “pneumonia of an as-yet unestablished origin” and say there is no epidemic risk (Reuters).
