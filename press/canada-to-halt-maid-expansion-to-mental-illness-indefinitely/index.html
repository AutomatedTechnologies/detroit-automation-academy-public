Canada to Halt MAID Expansion to Mental Illness Indefinitely

  • On Wednesday, October 7, 2026, Justice Minister Sean Fraser said Prime Minister Mark Carney’s government will introduce legislation this fall to indefinitely halt the expansion of medical assistance in dying (MAID) to people whose sole qualifying condition is mental illness, per the article.
  • Canada was set to become one of the few countries in the world to explicitly allow MAID for mental illness; an advisory committee recommended in June that the government not proceed with the expansion.
  • The article reports that 1 in 20 deaths in Canada is triggered by MAID, among the highest assisted-suicide rates in the world, and that eligibility requires a condition that is “intolerable” and cannot be relieved under conditions the person considers acceptable — not a terminal illness.
  • Fraser cited “very real concerns about people who live in communities with inadequate access to mental-health services” and a “lack of consensus over when mental illness is irremediable.”

Details and quotations are from the article’s accessible portion; the piece is paywalled, so the summary covers the full text the page exposes. The MAID figures are the article’s reporting, not independently verified. Original headline: "Canada Makes It Harder to Die, but There’s a Catch".

Removed: the characterization of Canada’s MAID trajectory (access moving “in one direction only,” MAID as “just another part” of the health-care system), the claim that the determination is “impossible,” the first-person reporting-history framing, and the subscription paywall pitch.
