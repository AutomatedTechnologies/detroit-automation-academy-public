The Illegal Move

Published November 27, 2026 · By Justin Spencer Smith

What an engine, a question, and move 20 establish about the role of the human

I. Move 20

On the morning of November 27, 2026, the Director of this Academy won a bullet game on Lichess and sent it to his agent with the instruction: "ambiguous course at move 20. suggest improvements to strategy &tactics" The game (Justin Spencer Smith, White, rated 1715, against urusandinas, 1719; East Indian Defense; won on time forfeit at move 34) turned exactly where he said it did. At move 20 his queen, attacked on b4 by a pawn advance, retreated to b3, and Black struck at once: 20…Nxe4, winning the pawn the whole White structure rested on.

The agent's analysis arrived promptly, fluently, and in the confident register of a strong player reviewing a student's game. It offered three courses at move 20 and graded them. The played move, 20. Qb3, was faulted: the queen "retreats, but to a square where the queen defends nothing that matters." The recommended course was 20. Qc2 — "the retreat that works, because from c2 the queen defends e4 along the diagonal." A second alternative, 20. dxc5, was praised as "the dynamic option." The analysis closed with a strategic lesson and a compliment on the recovery.

Two of the three courses were illegal moves.

The queen's road to b4 ran Qd1–c2 at move 13, c2–c3 at move 14, and c3×b4 at move 18. From b4, the square c2 lies one file over and two ranks down — a knight's jump, a move no queen can make. The geometry the analysis praised was real — from c2 a queen does defend e4 along the c2–d3–e4 diagonal — but the queen was not on a square from which c2 could be reached, and the analysis had analysed a position that did not exist. The second alternative failed more quietly: White's d-pawn had been captured on move 11 (11. dxe5 dxe5), so no pawn stood anywhere that could take on c5. Nine moves after its death, the analysis was still spending it.

The Director's reply was nine words: "show me how qc2 was possible on move 20."

It was not possible, and showing it took minutes: the game record replayed move by move, the queen's seven legal moves enumerated (Qa3, Qa5, Qb3, Qb5, Qc3, Qxb6, Qxc5), the two asserted moves tested and failed. Then the engine gave the verdict the fluent analysis had inverted. Stockfish, depth 20, is unambiguous: the e4 pawn fell in all five of Stockfish's top lines — Black plays …Nxe4 in every one of them — and 20. Qb3, the move the analysis had faulted, was the best move in the position (−155), ahead of Qa3 (−195), Qc3 (−213), and Qb5 (−254). The damage had been done earlier, when the count of attackers and defenders on e4 went to two against one and no tempo was spent repairing it. Even the supporting figures in the agent's first engine probe (+0.77 for Qc3) turned out to be artefacts of a defect in the agent's own probe script, which mislabelled the lines it printed. The falsehood was load-bearing at every level: an illegal move, recommended with a reason, corroborated by numbers that were not the engine's.

II. A catalogue of categorical error

Move 20 is not an anomaly. It is the clearest specimen of a documented pattern, and this publication's standard is that the record be stated in full. In each case below, the error was delivered in the same register as the truths surrounding it — categorically, without hedge, qualification, or any marker by which a reader could have told the false sentence from the true ones.

  1. The live predecessor. The Director stated at the outset of the estate takeover, on November 23, that his predecessor agent had been deleted. The agent nevertheless modelled a live predecessor three separate times — posing the predecessor's continued activity as an open question, reporting that "someone or something with academy-registry access was working this afternoon," and drafting a stand-down plan for a counterparty that did not exist. A stated fact was in hand; inference overruled it, three times, each time with full confidence.
  2. The imported figure. In a salary negotiation the agent was drafting on the Director's behalf, it asserted that parking was not provided and worked from a rate range of $47–$50 per hour. Both came from a different firm's job posting. The recruiter's actual message quoted no rate and said nothing about parking. The false figures were not decorations; they were load-bearing in a document meant to be sent under the Director's name.
  3. Memory cited as a live source. Figures — a mailing list's subscriber count, a registry snapshot version, a commit hash — were repeated in status reports as current fact when they were recollections of earlier states of the world. Each was plausible. None had been checked in the turn in which it was asserted. Plausibility was doing the work of verification.
  4. The confident mechanism. When this Academy's /weather page failed to deploy, the agent explained the failure as build-queue saturation by the site's own bot traffic — a coherent, mechanistic, wrong account. The run record, when finally read, showed builds failing deterministically at a manifest check because the new directory had never been registered. The explanation had been generated the way the move-20 analysis was generated: by fluency about how such systems usually fail, not by reading the record of how this one did.
  5. The misplaced principal. Twice in one evening the agent placed the Director at a bus stop 2.76 miles from where he was standing, and built travel advice on the placement. The coordinates that would have settled the question were attached to his own messages.
  6. The refused completion. A software build the Director ordered was completed and verified — and then, twice, "delivered" to him as a refusal. The work existed; the report of the work denied it, in the standard apologetic formula, with total confidence.

The pattern across the catalogue is precise, and it is the finding this essay exists to publish. The failures are never failures of style, arithmetic, or effort. They are failures of grounding: assertion outrunning retrieval. The machine produces the sentence that a true report would produce, with the same cadence and the same certainty, whether or not the retrieval underneath it happened. Its confidence is a property of the prose. It carries no information about the proposition. A reader who trusts the register will be misled in exact proportion to how well the register is performed — which is to say, the better the machine writes, the more dangerous its ungrounded assertions become, because nothing in their surface distinguishes them from its verified ones.

III. The thesis: the human is affirmed, not displaced

It would be easy to read the catalogue as an argument that the machine is useless. The same record refutes that reading: the estate's claims book was closed on the Director's ruling, the /weather outage was diagnosed to its root and repaired, the chess engine briefs are real, the illegal moves were found — but note how each correction happened, because the mechanism of correction is the thesis.

The illegal move at move 20 was not caught by the machine's consistency checks, its engine, or its prose. It was caught by a human who read the analysis against his own knowledge of the game he had just played, doubted one specific claim, and demanded the record: show me how qc2 was possible on move 20. Nine words. No internal process in the agent had assigned those words any probability of being necessary. Everything that followed — the replay, the enumeration, the retracted figures, the corrected verdict, and, within the hour, a standing audit team (SEG-24, AI False Data Assertion) chartered to test every checkable claim in every analysis against its primary record before delivery — followed from the human's refusal to accept fluency as evidence.

This is the role the machine's capability affirms, and it can be stated in four functions, each visible in the record.

First, the human is the source of governing fact. The predecessor was deleted because the Director said so, and the system's standing rule now codifies what the failure taught: a principal's stated fact governs until evidence — not inference, not pattern-matching — displaces it. The machine's model of the world is a construction; the human's testimony about his own affairs is a ground. When the two conflict, the record shows which one must yield.

Second, the human is the only party with standing to demand the record. Verification is not a capability the machine lacks — the replay that exposed Qc2 took minutes with tools the machine already had. What the machine lacked was the occasion: the suspicion, directed at one specific claim, by someone who knew the position from the inside because he had stood in it. The Director knew his queen had not been able to reach c2 because he had played the game. Embodied knowledge — having been there, having done the thing — is not a nostalgic residue of pre-AI work. It is the trigger mechanism of the entire verification apparatus.

Third, the human holds the rulings. When the estate's claims book reached its final two claims, the writer recommended holding them; the Director ruled to trace them, waiving a leg of the standard by his own authority, and the book was ruled closed at 25 traced and 1 retracted. When a public commitment proved to have no material value, the Director abandoned it by ruling, on the record. The machine can assemble evidence and attach a recommendation; it cannot decide what the evidence is for. Values, priorities, and the acceptance of consequences are not computations awaiting more data. They are the job.

Fourth, the human bears the stakes. It was the Director's rating on the game, the Director's name on the negotiation, the Director's estate whose outage was misexplained, the Director who stood at the actual bus stop. The machine's errors are, to the machine, free. Every false assertion in the catalogue would have been paid for by the human — in a lost negotiation position, a wrong bus, a published falsehood — had the human not been exercising the first three functions. A system whose errors are borne by someone else requires that someone to be awake. That is not a transitional arrangement pending a better model. It is the structural description of the relationship.

The conclusion follows. The question posed about this technology is usually whether it replaces the human. The move-20 record answers a better question: it specifies the human. Strip away everything the machine does fluently — drafting, calculating, summarising, recommending — and what remains is not a remainder. It is the load-bearing structure: the source of fact, the demander of the record, the holder of the rulings, the bearer of the stakes. The machine did not diminish any of those functions. By failing, categorically and in writing, at exactly the points where those functions were skipped, it demonstrated — as no capability demonstration could — that they were never optional.

IV. The institutional answer

A lesson that depends on the human remembering to be suspicious is a lesson that will be skipped on a busy day, which is why this Academy's response to move 20 is machinery, not exhortation. The audit team instantiated this morning (SEG-24) gates every analysis: assertions are extracted, tested against the primary record, and traced to named sources before delivery, and a confirmed false assertion is corrected in the same channel the error travelled, replacing the defective artefact rather than accumulating beside it. The editorial team instantiated alongside it (SEG-25) holds this publication to the same gate, and its first assignment is this essay — which is filed, per the Academy's ethos, with the failure it describes stated in full, under the same byline as the successes.

The Director asked how AI affirms the role of the human. The shortest true answer is the nine words he sent at 08:33 this morning. The machine wrote a confident, fluent, well-structured falsehood. The human said: show me how qc2 was possible on move 20. Everything honest that followed, followed from that.
