The Region Voted for the Bus

Published November 20, 2026

This was the week Detroit's transit future stopped being a proposal and started being a mandate. Macomb County voted to pay for the bus. The hearings on next year's new routes are held, and the window to be heard is still open. The Academy's daily paper went live. And the map that tracks it all told us, honestly, what it still can't see.

The region voted for the bus

On Tuesday, November 3, Macomb County voters renewed the tax that pays for SMART — 0.94 mills for 2027 through 2031, carried by about 65 percent of the vote, worth roughly $37 million in its first year. Wayne County passed its own ten-year levy in August. Oakland was already in. That makes Macomb the last piece: for the first time, all three counties in metro Detroit are fully in on public transportation, for at least the next five years. The same ballot backed the veterans millage at 79 percent and the zoo at 69 percent. The 2027 expansion the hearings are drawing is now funded on the far side of a vote, not a promise.

The 2027 map is still being drawn

SMART held its three public hearings on the proposed 2027 service changes this week: Monday, November 16, by Zoom; Tuesday the 17th at the Trenton Veterans Memorial Library; Wednesday the 18th at Westland City Hall. No decision has been announced; SMART said it will post a video of Monday's presentation. The part that matters most to a rider is still open: written comments are accepted through December 31 at PublicHearing@smartbus.org. Three new routes are proposed — 120 Fort Street, downtown to Flat Rock; 290 Newburgh; 295 Plymouth — with extensions and new weekend service on seven existing routes, phased in across April, June, and September 2027. SMART's new Line 500 between Hamtramck and downtown Detroit was reported November 10; a possible extension past Eight Mile toward Hall Road is a longer-term plan, not a service running today. The inbox is still open.

What we're telling SMART

The Academy's own written comments go in before the window closes, and they say five things. Adopt the package as proposed — the region voted to fund it. Make real-time data completeness a service standard: in our hourly checks this week, as many as one SMART bus in five in morning service — and nearly the whole fleet overnight — published its position with no route attached, a bus a rider can see but cannot name; the new routes should not launch until they launch labeled. Publish each phase's full schedule and stop data at least 60 days out, so rider tools are accurate on day one. On weekend service, set the span before the headways — shift workers ride at the edges of the day. And publish the comment record before the board decides, so the region can see what it said and what was heard. The full text of our comments runs with this edition's record.

The paper is live

The Periodical is up at detroitautomationacademy.com/Periodical/ — live for production testing as of the evening of Wednesday, November 18, verified against the served bytes: HTTP 200, 32,309 bytes, hash-matched to the build. It is the Academy's daily paper in the late-1800s form: monochrome, one front page, real local weather, real live transit, real Signal headlines, a Finance desk that prints market figures only — no picks, no advice, none of the publisher's private numbers — and a Games section carrying a playable daily crossword. The paper it is printed on is not a picture of paper. It is built, seeded, from five causes — chain lines, fiber, deckled edges, folds, ink in the sheet — after two prototypes were rejected on the texture. The design desk behind it, SH-8, was instantiated the same morning. It is live for production testing; its final verdict — the owner's own eyes on the served page — is still open. A paper that admits what is unfinished is a paper you can trust with the rest.

What the map can and can't tell you yet

Live Transit Detroit's hourly checks kept passing through this morning — 310 live vehicles at 7 a.m., DDOT clean on every batch, the feed fresh. The map is telling the truth about where the buses are. What it cannot always tell you yet is which route a SMART bus is running. The cause was traced this week to the source, not to our builder: Swiftly's vehicle feed is publishing a growing share of SMART vehicles with no trip assignment attached — positions live, route blank. The Academy emailed Swiftly Support about it on Thursday, November 19, and is awaiting a reply. The buses are still all there. The labels will follow when the feed does.

Software that refuses to die

Snowfell, the Academy's board application, cleared its rig test on the owner's own device this week — his verdict, Tuesday, November 17: it clears. The next build, test-9, is already at testing. Spectacle failed its rig test in the morning — an unclosable dialog, caught on the owner's screenshot — and shipped its fix, test-22, the same day; it awaits the owner's retest. Distro stands at thirteen entries, verified against the served catalog this week. And the site got its house in order, too: one header across all 72 pages, the Voter Guide delisted, the sitemap down 66 to 64, the standing check 145 of 145 — and a dead contact address scrubbed from 76 places across 18 pages. Old machines, old pages, same rule: count it in public or don't claim it.

Rulebook watch

For the robotics cohort: the official BIOBUZZ competition manual now stands at version TU09. This newsletter's baseline, checked in September, was TU01; it stood at TU08 a week ago. Eight team updates have landed since the baseline. No single update gets summarized here, because summaries are how teams get rules wrong. Read the current manual before your next build session, not the one you downloaded in September.

The close

A region voted to fund its buses. A paper went live that prints only what it can verify. A map admitted what it can't label yet, and a piece of software failed its test in the morning and fixed itself by the evening. That is the week's record: what ran, what didn't, and what is still open. The inbox for the 2027 map stays open through December 31. The paper is on the stand now. Read it, ride it, and tell us what we missed.
