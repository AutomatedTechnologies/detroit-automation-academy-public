The Signal · Relaunch Edition · October 2026

The Signal is now a transit newsletter.

Published October 11, 2026 · By Justin Spencer Smith · Also on LinkedIn

For its first run, The Signal covered automation, robotics, and the Detroiters building the future. Thirty-three subscribers, a handful of editions, modest numbers throughout. That was the academy's beat then. The academy's beat now is transit.

This is a repositioning, not a victory lap. The transit coverage is the newest thing we publish and the smallest sample in our data. What's real is the work behind it — and the work is what forced the shift.

The focus shift, explained

The academy started in robotics and automation: youth STEM cohorts, drone builds, the machines themselves. That work continues. But over the last month, the engineering reality changed. The bulk of the academy's build time moved to transit — specifically, SMART integration: all 45 suburban routes through the Transit API, a live vehicle proxy, line geometry drawn for the first two routes with the remaining 43 in the backfill queue. Not a side project. The main project.

When the work shifts, the publication follows. A newsletter about robotics, published by an organization spending its days on bus routes, would be fiction. So The Signal becomes what the academy actually does: the buses, the rails, the maps, and the people trying to move around this city.

The through-line hasn't changed. The robots taught us to verify before we trust — check the sensor reading, flag the uncertainty instead of burying it. A transit newsletter built that way covers the system as it actually runs, not as the brochure describes it. Schedules, reliability, and the gaps between the map and the street. Same standard, new beat.

Why transit, and why now

Because the work is already the beat. A live 3D map of every DDOT bus, drawn on the actual streets, with trip planning. The People Mover rendered down to its concrete deck. SMART's 45 routes coming onto the map. These aren't announcements; they're shipped, running, and being found — the transit posts are reaching ten times more people than follow the accounts that publish them. Strangers are finding the maps. That's the signal worth following.

What stays

Two editions from the archive survive the cut, because they were already about transit:

  • Every DDOT Bus, Live, in 3D — Now With Trip Planning (Oct 6): the Live Transit map, now with trip planning.
  • The Ride Is Free (Oct 1): the Detroit 2126 series opener, on fare-free transit and what it would take.

Everything else has been removed from the newsletter. The work happened; the archive just no longer pretends this is a generalist publication.

What comes next

Transit, monthly, from Detroit:

  • The system as it runs. DDOT, SMART, the People Mover — what the schedules say, what the street says, and where they disagree.
  • The maps we build. Live Transit Detroit is an ongoing project. New layers, new modes, new installments — documented here as they ship, with the build notes to prove it.
  • The future on rails. Detroit 2126 continues: what transit in this region could look like a century out, grounded in what's being built now.

The cadence stays monthly. The standard stays the same: facts first, sources shown, gaps flagged instead of filled.

Thirty-three subscribers. If you're one of them, you already know the drill. If you're here because a bus was late and you want to know why — welcome to the new beat.
