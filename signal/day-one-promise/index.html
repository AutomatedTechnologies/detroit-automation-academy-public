My Only Day 1 Promise

Published November 20, 2026

The Wall Street Journal reports GM has hired a search firm to find the next CEO. The public applications are already in: bring back Pontiac, resurrect the Firebird, a real truck under $25K, Super Cruise free on every trim.

Here is mine. It is one sentence long.

Once elected CEO, I promise to leave engineering decisions to competent engineers — not MBAs.

That is the whole platform, and the 6.2L V8 is the reason it needs saying.

597,571 trucks and SUVs — Silverados, Tahoes, Sierras, Yukons, Escalades, model years 2021 to 2024 — recalled because their engines can fail outright. GM's filing blames sediment in the oil galleries and crankshafts finished out of spec. An independent teardown found the quieter fact underneath: the previous engine, the L86, ran polymer-coated rod bearings. The L87 kept the coating on the mains and dropped it from the rods. The failures live exactly where the coating used to be.

Watch the teardown: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_-xhQ88Hlmc

A bearing coating costs cents per engine. Nobody saves cents per engine by accident; somebody priced that coating in a spreadsheet, and the spreadsheet won. The recall is the invoice — for every engine that fails GM's test, the remedy is a new engine. NHTSA has since opened an engineering analysis covering nearly a million 2021–2026 vehicles.

That is what an MBA decision looks like when it reaches a rotating assembly. The engineer who specced the coating knew what it was for. The review that removed it knew what it cost. Only one of those is an engineering fact.

So the promise, spelled out:

  • Any part that carries load, heat, or oil pressure keeps the spec its engineer signed. A cost review can ask the question; it cannot hold the pen.
  • A saving that only works if nothing ever goes wrong is not a saving. It is a recall with a later date.
  • The product questions — Pontiac, Blazers, cheap trucks — get answered in the open, by the people who design and build the vehicles, with the numbers on the page. Not by whoever writes the best wishlist.

GM does not need a CEO with a longer wishlist. It needs one who knows which decisions were never his to make.

Day 1: the engineers get their pen back.
